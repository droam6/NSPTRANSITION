Interior Painting Tips: How to Get a Professional Finish

A fresh coat of paint is one of the most cost-effective ways to transform the look and feel of your home. Whether you are refreshing a single room or repainting the entire house, the difference between an amateur finish and a professional result comes down to preparation, technique and the right materials. While many homeowners on the North Shore enjoy tackling small painting projects themselves, knowing the proper methods will save you time, money and frustration.

In this guide, our painting team shares the techniques and tips that professional painters use every day to achieve flawless interior walls, ceilings and trim throughout homes across Sydney.

Surface Preparation: The Foundation of a Great Finish

Professional painters will tell you that preparation accounts for at least 70 percent of the work involved in a quality paint job. Skipping or rushing the preparation stage is the single biggest mistake DIY painters make, and it always shows in the final result. Here is how to prepare your surfaces properly.

Filling Holes and Cracks

Start by inspecting every wall and ceiling for nail holes, screw holes, hairline cracks, dents and any other imperfections. Use a quality interior filler such as Selleys Spakfilla or Dunlop Wall and Ceiling Repair to fill each hole. Apply the filler with a flexible putty knife, slightly overfilling each hole to allow for shrinkage as the filler dries. For larger cracks, use a fibreglass mesh tape over the crack before applying filler to prevent the crack from reappearing.

Sanding

Once the filler is completely dry, sand each repaired area smooth using 120-grit sandpaper. For previously painted walls, a light sand with 180-grit sandpaper over the entire surface will remove any gloss and create a key for the new paint to adhere to. Always sand in the direction of the grain on timber surfaces. Wipe down all sanded surfaces with a damp cloth to remove dust before priming or painting.

Sugar Soap Wash

Before painting, wash all surfaces with sugar soap to remove grease, grime, fingerprints and any residue that could prevent the paint from adhering properly. This step is particularly important in kitchens and bathrooms where grease and moisture build up over time. Mix the sugar soap according to the manufacturer's directions, wash with a sponge or cloth, then rinse with clean water and allow the surfaces to dry completely.

Choosing the Right Paint Finish

Selecting the correct paint sheen for each room is just as important as choosing the colour. Different sheens have different levels of durability, washability and light reflection, making them suitable for different areas of your home.

Flat (Matt) Finish

Flat paint has no sheen and absorbs light, which makes it excellent at hiding minor wall imperfections like bumps, patches and uneven surfaces. It is the preferred choice for ceilings and low-traffic areas such as formal living rooms and bedrooms where walls are unlikely to need frequent washing. The downside is that flat paint is less durable and harder to clean, so it is not ideal for hallways, children's rooms or kitchens.

Low Sheen Finish

Low sheen is the most popular interior wall finish in Australian homes and is what we recommend for the majority of living spaces. It offers a subtle, soft glow that is easy on the eye while being significantly more washable and durable than flat paint. Low sheen works well in bedrooms, living rooms, dining rooms and hallways, making it a versatile all-rounder for homes across suburbs like Killara and the broader North Shore.

Semi-Gloss Finish

Semi-gloss paint has a noticeable sheen that reflects more light and is highly durable and washable. It is the best choice for wet areas like bathrooms, laundries and kitchens where walls are exposed to moisture, steam and splashes. Semi-gloss is also commonly used on skirting boards, architraves, door frames and window frames to create a clean contrast against the lower-sheen walls.

Gloss Finish

High-gloss paint offers the most reflective, hard-wearing finish and is typically reserved for interior doors, cabinetry, handrails and decorative mouldings. While it is extremely durable and easy to wipe clean, gloss paint highlights every imperfection in the surface beneath it, so thorough preparation and priming are essential.

Australian Paint Brands Worth Considering

Australia has several excellent paint manufacturers that produce products specifically formulated for our climate and conditions. Here are three brands our painting team regularly uses and recommends.

Dulux

Dulux is Australia's most recognised paint brand and offers an extensive range of interior products. Their Wash&Wear line is particularly popular for interior walls, offering excellent washability and coverage. Dulux also provides an industry-leading colour matching service and the widest colour range available in Australia. For premium finishes, their Dulux Professional range delivers superior results.

Haymes

Haymes is an Australian family-owned paint company known for producing high-quality, low-VOC paints that are better for indoor air quality. Their Ultra Premium range is a favourite among professional painters for its exceptional coverage, smooth application and durability. Haymes paints are particularly well-suited to homes where air quality is a priority, such as households with young children or allergy sufferers.

Taubmans

Taubmans is another trusted Australian brand with a strong reputation for value and performance. Their Endure range offers excellent coverage in fewer coats and is backed by a 15-year performance guarantee. Taubmans also offers a user-friendly online colour selection tool that makes choosing your palette simple and enjoyable.

Tools You Will Need

Having the right tools makes a significant difference to the quality of your finish. Here is what you will need for an interior painting project:

  • Rollers: Use a 270mm roller frame with 10mm nap microfibre sleeves for smooth walls and 15mm nap for textured surfaces. A smaller 100mm roller is useful for tight areas behind toilets and radiators.
  • Brushes: Invest in quality angled sash brushes (50mm and 63mm) for cutting in. Cheap brushes leave brush marks and shed bristles into your paint. Brands like Supercoat and Purdy are excellent choices.
  • Roller tray: Use a sturdy metal or heavy-duty plastic roller tray. Avoid flimsy trays that flex when you load the roller.
  • Drop sheets: Canvas drop sheets are far superior to plastic ones because they absorb drips and do not slip underfoot. Cover all floors, furniture and fittings thoroughly.
  • Masking tape: Use quality painters tape such as Frogtape or 3M ScotchBlue for clean, crisp lines along ceilings, skirting boards and around light switches.
  • Extension pole: An extendable roller pole allows you to paint walls and ceilings from the floor, reducing the need for ladders and improving your reach.
  • Sandpaper: Keep 120-grit and 180-grit sandpaper on hand for preparation and light sanding between coats.

Cutting In Technique

Cutting in is the technique of painting clean, straight lines along the edges of walls where they meet ceilings, skirting boards, door frames and window frames. It is one of the most skill-intensive aspects of interior painting and the area where DIY painters most commonly struggle.

To cut in effectively, load your angled sash brush with a moderate amount of paint and tap off the excess. Starting about 10mm away from the edge, press the brush lightly against the wall and slowly drag it towards the edge, letting the bristles fan out to create a straight line. Work in sections of about 600mm at a time, maintaining a wet edge. The key is to use long, smooth strokes and avoid overloading the brush, which causes drips and uneven lines.

Always cut in before rolling the main wall area. This allows the roller to blend into the cut-in edges while both are still wet, preventing visible lap marks. Professional painters, like our team working on homes across Neutral Bay and the lower North Shore, typically cut in an entire room before rolling for the most seamless result.

How Many Coats Do You Need?

The number of coats required depends on several factors, including the current wall colour, the new colour, the quality of the paint and whether a primer has been used.

  • Primer: Always apply a primer coat when painting over new plasterboard, patched areas, stains, dark colours or when making a dramatic colour change. A quality primer seals the surface and provides a uniform base for the topcoat.
  • Two topcoats: In most situations, two coats of premium paint will deliver a rich, even finish with full coverage. Apply the second coat only after the first coat has dried completely.
  • Three coats: You may need three coats when covering very dark colours with a lighter shade, when using reds, yellows or bright colours that are naturally less opaque, or when painting over a surface that is significantly different in texture or porosity.

Always allow each coat to dry fully before applying the next. Rushing between coats is a common mistake that leads to peeling, bubbling and an uneven finish.

Drying Times in Sydney's Humidity

Sydney's climate, particularly the humidity during summer months, can significantly affect paint drying times. Most interior acrylic paints are touch dry within one to two hours under ideal conditions (20-25 degrees Celsius with moderate humidity). However, during Sydney's humid summer months, drying times can extend to three to four hours or longer.

For the best results, aim to paint on days when the relative humidity is below 70 percent. Avoid painting on rainy days or when humidity is exceptionally high, as excess moisture can prevent the paint from curing properly and may lead to a tacky, uneven finish. If you need to paint during humid weather, use fans or air conditioning to improve air circulation and reduce drying times. Allow a minimum of four hours between coats during humid conditions, even if the paint feels dry to the touch.

Recoat times are typically longer than touch-dry times. Always check the paint can label for the manufacturer's recommended recoat time, which is usually two to four hours for water-based interior paints under normal conditions.

When to Hire a Professional vs DIY

There is no shame in tackling a small painting project yourself, and many homeowners find it satisfying to paint a feature wall or refresh a bedroom. However, there are situations where hiring a professional painter will deliver a significantly better result and may even save you money in the long run.

Consider DIY if:

  • You are painting a single room with simple, straight walls and standard ceiling height
  • The existing paint is in good condition and you are applying a similar colour
  • You have the time, patience and physical ability to prepare surfaces properly
  • You are comfortable working on a ladder for extended periods

Hire a professional if:

  • You are painting multiple rooms or the entire interior of your home
  • There is significant preparation work needed, including crack repair, plaster patching or stripping old wallpaper
  • You have high ceilings, stairwells or difficult-to-reach areas
  • You are making a dramatic colour change or using specialty finishes
  • The project involves lead paint (common in pre-1970 homes across the North Shore)
  • You need the work completed quickly to a deadline, such as before selling or tenanting a property
  • You want a flawless, showroom-quality finish throughout
