How Often Should You Repaint Your House? A Guide for Sydney Homeowners

February 8, 2026 • 7 min read

One of the most common questions we hear from homeowners across Sydney's North Shore is straightforward but surprisingly difficult to answer simply: how often should you repaint your house? The honest answer is that it depends on a number of factors, including whether you are talking about interior or exterior surfaces, the material of your walls, the quality of the previous paint job, and the specific conditions your home faces. This guide breaks down each of those factors so you can make an informed decision about when your home is due for a fresh coat.

Interior Repainting: Every 5 to 10 Years

As a general rule, interior walls in a well-maintained Sydney home should be repainted every 5 to 10 years. The wide range reflects the enormous variation in how different rooms are used. A formal living room or guest bedroom that sees light use may look perfectly fresh after a decade, while a busy family kitchen or children's bedroom might start showing wear within three to five years.

Ceilings typically last longer than walls because they are not subject to physical contact, but they can yellow over time, particularly in homes where cooking generates steam and airborne grease. Bathrooms and laundries are special cases where high humidity can cause paint to bubble, peel or develop mould if the wrong product was used. These rooms often need attention sooner than the rest of the house, particularly if they lack adequate ventilation.

Exterior Repainting: Every 7 to 15 Years

Exterior paintwork faces far harsher conditions than anything inside your home, and its lifespan varies considerably depending on the building material, the aspect of the wall and the quality of the coating system used. A well-prepared, professionally painted exterior using premium acrylic paint can last 12 to 15 years on protected surfaces. North and west-facing walls that cop direct afternoon sun will deteriorate faster, sometimes needing attention within 7 to 10 years.

Homes in suburbs closer to the harbour and coastline, such as Wahroonga and the surrounding upper North Shore, benefit from being further from salt air, which gives paint a longer lifespan. Properties closer to the water or in elevated, exposed positions face additional challenges from wind-driven salt and moisture that accelerate paint breakdown.

Signs Your House Needs Repainting

Rather than relying solely on a calendar, it pays to watch for the physical signs that your paintwork is reaching the end of its life. Here are the key indicators:

  • Peeling and flaking: This is the most obvious sign. Once paint begins to peel, moisture can reach the substrate beneath, accelerating damage. Peeling on exterior timber is particularly urgent because exposed wood can absorb water and begin to rot.
  • Fading: All paint fades over time, but when the colour difference between a protected area (under an eave, for example) and an exposed surface becomes noticeable, it is time to consider repainting.
  • Chalking: Run your hand across the painted surface. If it leaves a powdery residue on your fingers, the paint binder has broken down and the coating is no longer providing effective protection. This is especially common on rendered exterior walls.
  • Cracking: Fine hairline cracks in paint, sometimes called crazing, indicate that the coating has lost its flexibility. If left untreated, these cracks widen and allow water behind the paint film.
  • Bubbling: Bubbles or blisters in paint usually indicate moisture trapped beneath the surface. This is common in bathrooms and on exterior walls where water is penetrating from behind.

How Sydney's Climate Affects Your Paint

Sydney's climate presents a unique combination of challenges for painted surfaces. The UV index in Sydney is among the highest of any major city in the world, and prolonged ultraviolet exposure is the single biggest cause of exterior paint degradation. UV light breaks down the chemical bonds in paint, causing fading, chalking and eventually cracking.

Humidity is another significant factor, particularly during Sydney's warm, humid summers. High moisture levels can prevent paint from curing properly during application and can cause existing paintwork to soften and become susceptible to mould growth. For homes on the lower North Shore and harbour-facing suburbs, coastal salt air adds another layer of aggression, depositing fine salt crystals on surfaces that draw moisture and accelerate corrosion of both paint and the materials beneath it.

The combination of intense sun, humidity and occasional heavy rain means that paint systems in Sydney work harder than in many other Australian cities. This is why preparation and product selection are so critical to achieving a long-lasting result.

Different Surfaces and Their Paint Lifespan

The material your home is built from has a major influence on how long paint lasts:

  • Weatherboard (timber): 7 to 10 years exterior. Timber moves with temperature and humidity changes, and paint must be flexible enough to move with it. Acrylic paints perform better than oil-based on weatherboard for this reason.
  • Brick: 10 to 15 years exterior. Painted brick generally holds up well, but once you paint brick you commit to repainting it, as removing paint from brick is extremely difficult and expensive.
  • Cement render: 8 to 12 years exterior. Render is porous and needs a quality sealer or primer before painting. Hairline cracks in render can telegraph through the paint and require filling before recoating.
  • Timber trim (fascias, window frames, doors): 5 to 8 years exterior. Trim gets the worst of the weather because it protrudes from the building, and horizontal surfaces like window sills collect water. Timber trim is almost always the first part of an exterior paint job to need redoing.
  • Colorbond or metal surfaces: 10 to 20 years. Factory-applied coatings on metal roofing and cladding last a long time, but once they begin to fade or chalk, a specialist metal primer and topcoat system is needed.

High-Traffic Areas That Need More Frequent Painting

Inside your home, certain areas wear out faster than others due to everyday use. Hallways and staircases are the most common culprits, particularly in family homes where walls get bumped by bags, scuffed by shoes and marked by fingerprints on a daily basis. Children's bedrooms and playrooms are another area where paint takes a beating, both from physical contact and from the occasional crayon or texta mark.

Kitchens accumulate grease and steam that can dull and discolour paint, particularly on the ceiling and walls nearest the cooktop. Using a washable, semi-gloss or satin finish in these high-traffic zones makes cleaning easier and extends the time between repaints. For homes in Pymble and across the upper North Shore where families with children are common, choosing durable, scrubbable paint finishes in busy areas is a smart investment.

How Paint Quality Affects Longevity

The difference between a premium paint and a budget product is not just marketing. Higher-quality paints contain a greater concentration of binders and pigments, which translates directly to better coverage, richer colour, improved adhesion and significantly longer life. A premium exterior acrylic from brands like Dulux Weathershield, Taubmans Endure or Haymes SolexShield will typically last two to four years longer than a budget alternative, which often makes the modest extra cost per litre well worthwhile when you factor in the labour cost of repainting sooner.

Primer is equally important and often overlooked by DIY painters. A quality primer seals the substrate, provides a uniform base for the topcoat and dramatically improves adhesion. Skipping primer to save time and money is one of the most common reasons paint fails prematurely.

Colour Fading: Which Colours Last Longest

Not all paint colours age equally. As a general rule, lighter colours and earth tones retain their appearance longer than deep, saturated shades. Whites, creams, beiges and light greys are the most fade-resistant options for exterior surfaces. Dark colours, particularly reds, blues and deep greens, absorb more UV radiation and heat, which accelerates pigment breakdown. This does not mean you should avoid dark exteriors, but it is worth knowing that a deep charcoal or navy facade will likely show fading sooner than a lighter alternative and may need recoating a year or two earlier.

The Cost of Waiting Too Long

One of the most important things to understand about repainting is that it is far cheaper to repaint on time than to repair the damage caused by waiting too long. When exterior paint fails and moisture reaches the timber beneath, you are no longer looking at a straightforward repaint. You are potentially facing timber replacement, filling of rotted sections, treatment of mould-affected framing and, in severe cases, waterproofing remediation.

A straightforward exterior repaint of a typical North Shore home might cost between $8,000 and $20,000 depending on size and complexity. Add in timber repairs from years of neglected paintwork, and that figure can easily double. Interior paint failure is less catastrophic but still costly, as water-damaged plasterboard may need replacing before new paint can be applied. The message is clear: regular maintenance painting is an investment that protects a far more valuable asset.

Getting Quotes: What to Look for in a Painting Quote

When you decide it is time to repaint, obtaining multiple quotes is standard practice. But not all quotes are created equal. A good painting quote should clearly specify the brand and product being used (not just "two coats of acrylic"), the preparation work included (washing, scraping, sanding, filling, priming), which areas are included and excluded, the number of coats for each surface, and the expected timeline. Be cautious of quotes that are significantly lower than others, as the savings usually come from cutting corners on preparation or using inferior products, both of which will cost you more in the long run.

Check that the painter is licensed, carries public liability insurance and is willing to provide references from recent jobs. A professional painter should be happy to walk you through their proposed process and explain why they are recommending specific products for your home.
