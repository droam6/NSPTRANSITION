End of Lease Cleaning Checklist: What Sydney Renters Need to Know

Moving out of a rental property in Sydney can be stressful, and one of the biggest concerns for tenants is getting their bond back in full. In New South Wales, landlords and property managers can claim part or all of your bond if the property is not returned in a reasonably clean condition, accounting for fair wear and tear. The key to a smooth handover and full bond return is a thorough end of lease clean that meets the expectations set out in your condition report.

This comprehensive checklist covers every room and area that real estate agents and property managers inspect during a final walkthrough. Whether you are moving out of a studio apartment in North Sydney or a family home in Chatswood, this guide will help you understand exactly what needs to be cleaned and to what standard.

NSW Rental Bond Cleaning Requirements

Under the NSW Residential Tenancies Act 2010, tenants are required to leave the property in a reasonably clean condition, consistent with the condition report completed at the start of the tenancy. This does not mean the property needs to be in perfect condition; reasonable wear and tear from normal use is expected and accepted. However, general cleanliness, removal of all personal belongings and addressing any damage beyond normal wear are your responsibilities as a tenant.

It is important to note that landlords cannot require you to use a specific cleaning company or have carpets professionally steam cleaned unless this was the condition of the property when you moved in. However, if the carpets were professionally cleaned before your tenancy began (as noted on the condition report), you will likely need to have them professionally cleaned when you leave. Always refer to your original condition report as the benchmark for the standard expected at move-out.

Room-by-Room Cleaning Checklist

Kitchen

The kitchen is one of the most scrutinised areas during a final inspection because it accumulates the most grease, grime and food residue over time. Pay particular attention to these areas:

  • Clean the oven inside and out, including racks, trays, the glass door (between the glass panels if accessible) and the grill element
  • Degrease the rangehood, including filters, and wipe down the exterior
  • Clean the stovetop thoroughly, removing all burnt-on food and grease from burners and drip trays
  • Wipe down all benchtops, splashbacks and tile grout lines
  • Clean inside and outside all cupboards and drawers, removing crumbs and shelf liner residue
  • Clean the dishwasher interior, including the filter, door seals and detergent dispenser
  • Wipe down the refrigerator space (if fridge has been removed) or clean the fridge inside and out if it is included with the property
  • Clean the sink and taps, removing any limescale buildup
  • Wash or replace any grease-stained exhaust fan covers
  • Clean light fittings and light switches
  • Mop the floor, paying attention to corners and under the kickboards

Bathrooms and Toilets

Bathrooms require detailed attention to soap scum, mould, limescale and grout lines. Agents will inspect these areas closely:

  • Scrub and descale the shower recess, including walls, floor, glass screen or curtain rail, and shower head
  • Remove all mould from grout lines, silicone seals, ceilings and corners
  • Clean the bathtub inside and out, including any soap holders or ledges
  • Clean the toilet bowl, seat, base, cistern and behind the toilet
  • Clean the vanity, basin, taps and mirror
  • Wipe inside the vanity cupboard and drawers
  • Clean the exhaust fan cover and vent
  • Wipe down towel rails, hooks and toilet roll holders
  • Clean all tiles, removing soap scum and watermarks
  • Mop the floor, ensuring corners and behind the door are included

Bedrooms

  • Vacuum or mop all flooring, including under beds and in corners
  • Clean built-in wardrobes inside and out, including shelves, hanging rails and sliding tracks
  • Wipe down all skirting boards, door frames and architraves
  • Clean windows inside and out (if accessible), including tracks and sills
  • Clean light fittings, light switches and power points
  • Remove any picture hooks and fill holes with white filler if required by your lease agreement
  • Clean ceiling fans and air conditioning vents or filters
  • Spot clean walls to remove scuff marks and fingerprints

Living Areas and Dining Rooms

  • Vacuum carpets thoroughly or mop hard floors
  • Clean skirting boards, cornices and architraves
  • Wipe down all window sills, tracks and frames
  • Clean windows inside and out where accessible
  • Dust and clean light fittings, ceiling fans and air conditioning vents
  • Clean fireplace surround and mantle if applicable
  • Remove cobwebs from all corners and ceilings
  • Spot clean walls and remove any adhesive residue from hooks or tape
  • Clean sliding door tracks and clean glass doors

Laundry

  • Clean the laundry tub and taps, removing any stains or soap residue
  • Wipe down benchtops and splashbacks
  • Clean inside and outside laundry cupboards
  • Clean behind and under the washing machine space
  • Clean the dryer vent and lint filter area
  • Mop the floor and clean skirting boards
  • Wipe down any wall-mounted drying racks or hooks

Outdoor Areas

  • Sweep balconies, patios and courtyards
  • Remove cobwebs from eaves, railings and outdoor light fittings
  • Clean outdoor furniture if it belongs to the property
  • Remove all personal items from garden sheds or storage cages
  • Ensure garden beds are tidy and lawns are mowed if garden maintenance is your responsibility
  • Clean any barbecue areas
  • Sweep garage floors and remove oil stains if possible

Common Areas Tenants Miss

Even with the best intentions, there are several areas that tenants frequently overlook during an end of lease clean. These are the spots that experienced property managers check first and that often result in bond deductions:

  • Inside the oven: By far the most common item flagged during inspections. A surface wipe is not enough; the oven needs to be free of grease, carbon buildup and burnt food residue on all interior surfaces.
  • Rangehood filters: Greasy, clogged rangehood filters are easy to forget but immediately noticeable during an inspection.
  • Window tracks and sills: Dirt, dust and dead insects accumulate in window tracks over time. A vacuum and wipe with a damp cloth will address this.
  • Top of cupboards and wardrobe doors: Dust settles on surfaces above eye level that are rarely noticed during everyday living.
  • Skirting boards: Dusty or scuffed skirting boards stand out, particularly in empty rooms where they are fully exposed.
  • Light fittings and ceiling fans: Dust and insect buildup on light covers and fan blades are common inspection items.
  • Behind the toilet: The area behind and around the base of the toilet is often overlooked but is always checked.
  • Sliding door and window tracks: Built-up grime in tracks is a frequent issue, especially in properties near the harbour or coast where salt air accelerates buildup.

Oven Cleaning Tips

The oven is often the most challenging part of an end of lease clean. Here are some effective methods for getting your oven spotless:

Commercial oven cleaner: Products like Easy-Off or Coles Ultra Oven Cleaner are highly effective at dissolving baked-on grease. Spray the interior generously, close the door and leave for the time recommended on the product (usually 20-30 minutes). Then scrub with a non-scratch scouring pad and wipe clean. You may need to repeat the process for heavily soiled ovens.

Baking soda paste: For a less chemical approach, make a thick paste of baking soda and water. Spread it over all interior surfaces of the oven, avoiding the heating elements, and leave overnight. The next morning, spray with white vinegar (it will fizz), then scrub and wipe clean. This method is effective for moderate grease buildup but may not tackle heavy carbon deposits.

Oven racks: Remove oven racks and soak them in hot water with dishwashing liquid in the bathtub or a large tub overnight. Scrub with a scouring pad the next day. For stubborn grime, sprinkle baking soda on the racks while soaking.

Window and Blind Cleaning

Clean windows make an immediate impression during an inspection. Use a quality glass cleaner or a solution of white vinegar and water (equal parts) applied with a spray bottle. Wipe with a microfibre cloth or squeegee for a streak-free finish. Clean both sides of the glass where accessible, and do not forget the window frames, tracks and sills.

For venetian blinds, close the blinds and wipe each slat with a damp microfibre cloth, then flip and repeat on the other side. Roller blinds can be wiped down with a damp cloth or vacuumed with a brush attachment. If the blinds are heavily stained or damaged, check your condition report to determine if they were in good condition at the start of your tenancy.

Carpet Cleaning Requirements

Carpet cleaning is one of the most debated aspects of end of lease cleaning in NSW. Here is what you need to know:

If the carpets were professionally steam cleaned before your tenancy (and this is noted on the condition report), you will generally be expected to have them professionally cleaned when you leave. Ask the property manager for a copy of the original condition report to verify this. If the carpets were not professionally cleaned before your tenancy, you are only required to return them in a similar condition, accounting for normal wear.

When booking professional carpet cleaning, ensure the company provides a receipt or invoice that includes their business name, ABN, the date of the clean and the address. Keep this receipt as evidence in case of any bond dispute. Hot water extraction (steam cleaning) is the most commonly accepted method for end of lease carpet cleaning.

For spot stains, try treating them before the professional clean. Blot (do not rub) spills immediately with a clean white cloth, and use a carpet stain remover appropriate for the type of stain. Common stains like red wine, coffee and pet stains may require professional treatment.

Timeline: When to Start Cleaning Before Inspection

Timing your end of lease clean correctly is important. Start too early and the property may get dusty again before the inspection. Leave it too late and you risk running out of time to address any issues the agent raises. Here is a recommended timeline:

  • Two weeks before move-out: Begin decluttering and packing non-essential items. Book professional carpet cleaning and any other specialist services (oven cleaning, window cleaning) you plan to outsource. Arrange for professional cleaners if you are hiring an end of lease cleaning service.
  • One week before: Continue packing and start cleaning rooms that are already empty or nearly empty. Clean wardrobes, cupboard interiors and storage areas as you empty them. Begin outdoor area maintenance.
  • Two to three days before: Complete the main clean of the property once most furniture and belongings have been removed. This is when you tackle kitchens, bathrooms, floors, walls and windows. Having the property as empty as possible makes cleaning much more thorough and efficient.
  • Day before inspection: Do a final walkthrough using this checklist. Touch up any areas that need attention, vacuum and mop floors one last time, and ensure all personal items have been removed. Check light bulbs are all working and replace any that have blown.
  • Day of inspection: Arrive 30 minutes early to do a final check. Open windows to air the property, ensure all lights are on and the property looks its best. Have your cleaning receipts (carpet cleaning, professional cleaning) ready to present to the agent.

Professional vs DIY End of Lease Cleaning

One of the biggest decisions you will face is whether to tackle the end of lease clean yourself or hire professional cleaners. Both approaches have their advantages.

DIY cleaning saves money and gives you full control over the process. It works well if you are comfortable with detailed cleaning, have the time to do a thorough job, and the property is relatively small and in good condition. However, it requires investing in cleaning supplies and equipment, and there is no guarantee of passing the inspection on the first attempt.

Professional end of lease cleaning provides peace of mind and significantly reduces the stress of moving. Reputable cleaning companies, like our team at North Shore Projects, bring commercial-grade equipment, professional cleaning products and the experience to know exactly what agents look for. The biggest advantage of hiring professionals is the bond-back guarantee, meaning if the agent finds any issues with the clean, the cleaning company will return to rectify them at no additional cost.

For busy professionals, families with young children, or anyone moving under time pressure, professional cleaning is almost always worth the investment. The cost of a professional end of lease clean is typically far less than the amount you could lose from your bond if the property fails inspection.
